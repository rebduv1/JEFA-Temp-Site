How it works

Three steps from your taxes to their tuition.

Donate to JEFA

Contribute any amount up to $1,700 to the Jewish Education Fund of America, a registered 501(c)(3) Scholarship Granting Organization.

Claim your credit

Take a 100% nonrefundable federal tax credit for the full amount when you file. If you can’t use it all this year, carry it forward for up to five years.

Scholarships are awarded

JEFA grants at least 90% of contributions directly to eligible K-12 students at Jewish day schools and yeshivas across the country.

The numbers

$1,700*

A credit is not a deduction.

A deduction lowers your taxable income — worth a few hundred dollars at most. A credit lowers your tax bill itself. Owe $5,000 and claim a $1,700 credit? You now owe $3,300, and the full $1,700 funds a Jewish child’s education.

*Maximum annual credit per taxpayer under IRC §25F, signed into law July 4, 2025.

Questions

Before you ask.

Does this actually cost me anything?

No. Every dollar you contribute to JEFA reduces your federal tax bill by exactly one dollar. You are choosing where up to $1,700 of your existing tax liability goes — to the IRS, or to Jewish education.

What is a Scholarship Granting Organization?

An SGO is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that accepts donations and uses at least 90% of its income to fund K-12 scholarships for eligible students. JEFA is a registered SGO dedicated to Orthodox Jewish children’s religious education.

Does my state need to opt in?

Not for you to donate — any taxpayer nationwide can contribute to a qualified SGO in a participating state and claim the credit. For students in your state to receive scholarships, your state must opt in. As of early 2026, 30 states have done so, and the list is growing.

Can I give less than $1,700?

Absolutely. Contribute any amount up to $1,700 and receive a dollar-for-dollar credit for whatever you give. A $500 gift means $500 less in taxes owed and $500 toward a child’s Torah education.
